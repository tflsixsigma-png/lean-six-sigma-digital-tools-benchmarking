The two sessions

One argument runs across the day. The morning builds it from operations: efficiency is a consequence of strategic choice and of flow, not of utilisation. The afternoon tests it against what money can actually buy — digital platforms and other organisations' practices.

No preparation is required. Both sessions stand on their own. These notes carry the definitions, figures and sources, so the time in the room can be spent on judgement rather than terminology.

What to have in mind

Nothing to prepare, but three things worth having ready — all about your own organisation, all answerable from what you already know: one recurring process that takes longer than it should, which the morning will map; two rough figures — projects in flight, and projects closed in the last twelve months; and one authorised project you know well enough to defend, which is the raw material for the group activity that closes the morning.

The instrument

One tool, twenty minutes. Does this change what the organisation owns or what it is able to do; where the tool contains AI, what has the system been told to want, to know and to see; and at which link does the chain from a working model to an organisational result actually break?
