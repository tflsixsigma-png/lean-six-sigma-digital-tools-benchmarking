Objectives

The session closes Module 3 — Methods, Tools and Operational Efficiency — by asking where operational efficiency actually comes from. One argument runs across the day: efficiency is a consequence of strategic choice and of flow, not of utilisation, and the capability that produces it cannot be bought. The morning builds the argument from operations; the afternoon tests it against the two things organisations buy when they want capability and cannot build it — technology, and other people's practices.

By the end of the day participants should be able to:

UnderstandExplain value from the customer backwards (V = C × (A + P)), the five Lean principles, the eight wastes, the efficiency paradox and the logic of Six Sigma as variation reduction. DiagnoseMap a value stream (process time, lead time, %C&A), read a control chart for common and special cause, and place a digital tool on the six-condition chain from a working model to an organisational result. ApplyRun a DMAIC improvement cycle, use Little's Law to size portfolio lead time, trace a lead-time reduction to ROIC, and conduct a benchmarking study with Camp's method. JudgeDecide whether a digital investment changes what the organisation owns or what it is able to do; audit an AI system for philosophical debt; and choose which capability gaps to close first, and in what order, under Cynefin and Kata.

Morning — Lean Six Sigma & Continuous Improvement

Efficiency, waste reduction and performance optimisation. Examinable concepts:

  1. VCAP — V = C × (A + P) — and the value stick
  2. The five Lean principles and the eight wastes
  3. Value stream mapping: process time, lead time, %C&A
  4. The efficiency paradox: resource vs flow efficiency
  5. Process capability (Cp/Cpk) and common vs special cause
  6. DMAIC as the method for conducting improvement
  7. Little's Law and the effect of lead time on ROIC

Afternoon — Digital Tools & Benchmarking

Integration of digital platforms and international best practice. Examinable concepts:

  1. What a digital investment buys: ownership vs capability
  2. Philosophy eats AI — teleology, epistemology, ontology — and philosophical debt
  3. The six-condition chain from a working model to net value
  4. Benchmarking method (Camp) and comparator types
  5. Why best practice resists transfer: stickiness and causal ambiguity
  6. Cynefin and Toyota Kata as the transformation vector
  7. Operational Excellence = Design(VCAP) × Culture

Method

Each half-day combines plenary exposition with short applied exercises and one structured group activity, so that the concepts are used on the participants' own organisations within the same session. The theory is introduced deductively and immediately tested inductively against examples the participants bring.

No preparation is required. The reading notes for both sessions are delivered with the class, carry every definition, figure and source, and are written to stand on their own — they remain the primary reference for the module assessment. Participants are asked only to have in mind one recurring process in their organisation that takes longer than it should, two rough figures (projects in flight and projects closed in the last twelve months), and one digital tool they are evaluating or have already bought.

Structured activities

Morning · The Value Case Canvas

In groups, on one authorised project a member knows well enough to defend. Six steps: the project and the competitive priority it affects; the WTP edge — does the customer notice?; a map of the stream it touches (process time, lead time, where the gap sits); the cost edge — the waste removed; what the project actually builds in A, P and C, and how long a competitor would need to replicate it; and the verdict.

Fillable online and printed in the reading notes. Twenty-five minutes, then readouts.

Afternoon · The Digital Tool Appraisal

In groups, on one tool a member is evaluating or has already bought. In the room: VCAP triage (does it change what the organisation owns or what it is able to do?), the six-condition maturity chain, and — where the tool contains AI — a philosophical debt audit on the three questions, ending in a ranked verdict. After the session: a benchmarking reference and a six-month roadmap across the three capability gaps.

Fillable, exportable instrument, also printed as a booklet. Thirty-five minutes, then readouts.

Execution Optimization Challenge. Module 3's structured activity (3 h, group) is run by the module coordination. Both canvases produce material of the kind the Challenge draws on — a mapped value stream, a quantified lead-time case, and an appraised digital tool — and groups are encouraged to keep their completed worksheets for it.

Assessment

Module 3 is assessed through the Execution Optimization Challenge, a group assignment, and through the written assessment prepared by the module coordination on the basis of the session materials. The reading notes are therefore the reference text: every examinable concept listed under Objectives is defined, illustrated and sourced in them.

Evaluation criteria, as set for the programme:

  • Conceptual understanding — the definitions are used correctly and the argument that links them is reproduced, not just the vocabulary.
  • Practical application — the tools are applied to a real process, project or digital investment, with figures.
  • Strategic coherence — the improvement proposed is traced to the value term it moves and to the competitive advantage it serves.
  • Clarity of communication — the case can be read and defended by someone who was not in the room.
