Figure 1 · The triage, applied to a tool

Part Two · V = C × (A + P)

Almost every platform is an A decision; some are also P; very few are C. The error that destroys the business case is not buying A — it is booking the benefit as though C had moved.

Figure 2 · The six-condition chain

Part Four · from the author's systematic review (PRISMA 2020)

Organisational value is the end of a chain. Conditions 2 and 3 — whether the output reaches whoever decides while the decision is still open, and whether anyone can act — are not properties of the tool. They are capabilities of the organisation that bought it, and where the evidence says value is most often lost.

Figure 3 · Cynefin — the domain decides the method

Part Six · after Snowden & Boone (2007)

Most leaders are trained for the Complicated domain. Digital transformation is Complex, and applying Complicated methods there manufactures a sense of control that delays experimentation and penalises learning. Benchmarking is a Simple-and-Complicated instrument.

Figure 4 · Toyota Kata — the routine

Part Six · Rother (2009)

Probe → sense → respond is a logic, not a practice. Kata is the routine: a direction, the current condition, a target condition within three months, and daily experiments towards it — with a coach asking the same five questions every cycle.

Figure 5 · Capability as a stock

Part Eight · after Dierickx & Cool (1989)

A stock with a regulated inflow and an unregulated outflow. The valve is psychological safety and the credibility of management's commitments: where surfacing a problem is costly, the measured performance improves and the real performance does not.

Figure 6 · Know-why × know-how

Part Eight · after Lapré, Mukherjee & Van Wassenhove (2000)

Only the improvement projects that produced an understanding of the mechanism and validated it in the work accelerated the reduction of waste. Volume of projects is not the measure.

On Monday · bridges to project management

The afternoon tests the morning's identity against the two things organisations buy when they cannot build capability: technology and other people's practices. Each part ends with a bridge to project management and one thing to do on Monday.

The equations of the afternoon

V = C × (A + P)

A platform enters the bracket; the coefficient is bought separately, or not at all. A is bought · P is copied · C is the only term you have to build.

Vrival = Cweak × (Abought + Pcopied) ≈ 0

The arithmetic of failed benchmarking: GM copied the bracket at NUMMI for twenty years and could not import the coefficient.

VC = (WTP − C)A − (WTP − C)B

A successful imitation pulls the second bracket up towards the first. Benchmarking practised well by everyone converges an industry on one operating model — Porter's caveat.

OpEx = Design(VCAP) × Culture

What the organisation specified, multiplied by whether it is lived. A reminder, not a measurement — the product form forbids the claim that a strong design offsets a weak practice.

Bridges to project management — and what to do on Monday

Part Two · What a platform buys

Every fact has one home

A tool used as the working record by 40% of the people it was bought for delivers close to none of a single-source benefit. A date, a risk or a cost that lives in two systems imposes a permanent reconciliation tax and turns the dashboard into a negotiation.

On Monday List the five facts leadership asks about most; for each, write where the authoritative version lives. If any has two homes, that is the first project — and it is not a software project. Put change management on the same budget line as licences.
Part Three · Philosophy eats AI

The question for the next steering committee

Before granting any system autonomy, technical safeguards are necessary and not sufficient. Three philosophical questions decide whether autonomy is efficiency or risk in costume.

On Monday Teleology: is the hierarchy of purposes documented, including what to do when cost, quality and sustainability conflict? Epistemology: does it know when it does not know, and escalate? Ontology: can it recognise a supplier or a context it has never seen?
Part Four · The chain

Conditions 2 and 3 are almost never in scope

The system is delivered, and the decision it enters and the capacity to act on it are left to change management after go-live. Condition 4 is benefits realisation — associated with project success, rarely performed.

On Monday For the digital project closest to go-live, write down who receives the output, at which decision, and who acts on it with what resources. If any answer is "not defined", that is the gap — and it is the organisation's, not the vendor's.
Part Five · Benchmarking

Benchmark the routine, not the number

Do not ask the reference what it does; ask what it stopped doing and what it would never give up. Then ask what your organisation would have to accumulate to run that routine — and how long the reference took.

On Monday Take one benchmark figure your organisation quotes. Check whether the process boundaries and the conditions are the same on both sides; then name the practice that produces the gap. If nobody can, the figure is a datum, not a target.
Part Six · Cynefin & Kata

A gate asks for a plan; a Complex problem can only supply a hypothesis

So the plan gets written anyway, and the organisation manages the plan instead of the problem — holding people to dates that were never knowledge, only politeness.

On Monday Rewrite one gate deliverable in this form: "By day 90, this measure will read this value; here is what the last four weeks showed." If the sponsor will not accept that in place of a plan, the problem is being governed as Complicated, and the next quarter will prove it.
Part Eight · Building the multiplier

Retrospectives that change the standard

The retrospective is guided experimentation if its output changes the standard, and nothing if it does not. The lessons-learned repository stores what can be written down — the part that was never the constraint.

On Monday Open the last three retrospectives and look for one line in a template, a checklist or a gate criterion that changed because of them. If there is none, they produced know-why without know-how, and the stock did not move.

The dilemma · a competitor cuts cost by 30% with generative AI

01

Cynefin. Is this Simple — copy the technology — or Complex — discover how AI could create value specific to your customer?

02

Lean. Is human contact a driver of willingness to pay in your segment? If it is, automating it cuts cost and destroys revenue at the same time.

03

Kata. Set a ninety-day target condition and run controlled experiments towards it, rather than approving a programme whose benefits nobody can yet state.

What to expect

"Not documented" will be the honest answer on what the system optimises for. Most tools land on A, or A and P — a correctly identified purchase, so argue the case on that basis. And the six conditions will be answered "not measured" more often than "no": an unobserved implementation, not a failing one — a more tractable problem.
